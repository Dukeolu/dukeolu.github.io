BA Case 01 · Process Improvement

Cutting AP invoice approval time without cutting corners

Process MappingRoot-Cause AnalysisExcel

Brightpath Distribution (simulated) routes every invoice of $500 or more through the same three sequential approvals — department manager, finance manager, controller — regardless of whether it's a $600 supply order or a $60,000 purchase. Finance's complaint was vague: "invoices take too long, and vendors are pushing back on our payment terms." This case measures where the time actually goes and what fixing it is worth.

Avg cycle time
12.8 → 4.3 days
Reduction
66%

22% → 80% target

Approach

  1. 01

    Discovery — Pulled AP timestamps and approver email logs for one month (120 invoices) to build a time-in-stage log across data entry and each approval tier.

  2. 02

    Root-cause analysis — 5 Whys, tracing "invoices take ~13 days" back to a three-approval policy that was never revisited as invoice volume tripled.

  3. 03

    Future-state design — Tier approvals by invoice amount, add OCR/auto-capture for recurring vendors, replace untracked email routing with a workflow tool that has reminders and backup approvers.

  4. 04

    Validation — Modeled the future state row-by-row against the same 120-invoice sample, not just as a blended average.

Key finding

The 12.8-day average isn't one bottleneck — it's four moderate ones stacked in sequence that nobody had measured together before: 1.3 days of manual re-keying, then roughly 4, 3.6, and 4.2 days sitting in three separate inboxes. The three-approval chain applies to 82% of invoices today; under a tiered redesign, 83% of invoices (everything under $10,000) would clear in a single approval instead of three. The direct cost: this one-month sample alone forfeited $10,401 in early-payment discounts — only 22% of discount-eligible invoices actually captured their discount.

Recommendation & business impact

Tier approvals by dollar amount, deploy OCR for the ~75% of volume from recurring vendors, and replace email routing with a workflow tool that has visibility and defined backup approvers — implemented together. Modeled against the same invoice population: average cycle time drops from 12.8 to 4.3 days (a 66% reduction), recovering an estimated $124,815/year in discounts. Against a $49,600 combined tool and implementation cost, that's a net benefit of ~$75,000 in year one and ~$97,000 every year after — paying for itself in under five months.

The pitch in one line: The policy wasn't updated when the company outgrew it — three years of "we've always done it this way" is quietly costing $125K a year in discounts nobody's collecting.
