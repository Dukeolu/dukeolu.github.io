BA Case 02 · Requirements & Systems Selection

Choosing a CRM the sales team will actually use

BRDRequirements MatrixVendor Evaluation

A 40-person sales team at Brightpath Distribution (simulated) has quietly abandoned its 9-year-old CRM for personal spreadsheets. The loudest request from leadership was "buy us a modern CRM" — that's a solution, not a problem statement. Before any vendor demo, this case does the unglamorous work: documenting what the business actually needs, then scoring three real candidate vendors against it instead of against whichever one gave the best demo.

Approach

  1. 01

    Elicitation — Structured interviews with sales reps, sales management, and finance to separate "must have" from "would be nice," surfacing requirements nobody had written down (like territory-based pipeline visibility and quote-to-invoice handoff).

  2. 02

    Requirements documentation — 14 functional requirements (MoSCoW-prioritized) and 5 non-functional requirements, each traced forward to which vendor capability satisfies it.

  3. 03

    Vendor evaluation — Scored three real-world-comparable CRM platforms against weighted criteria (functional fit, total cost, implementation risk, vendor viability) rather than a single "which felt better in the demo" impression.

  4. 04

    Total cost of ownership — Built out licensing, implementation, and training costs over 3 years per vendor, not just year-one sticker price.

Key finding

The vendor with the flashiest demo (Pinnacle Sales Cloud) scores lowest once cost and implementation risk are weighted in — its 3-year TCO runs highest of the three, and its go-live timeline carries the most schedule risk. Northstar CRM wins on the weighted scorecard (4.22 vs. 4.01 for Vertex Sales Suite and 3.57 for Pinnacle) while also being the cheapest 3-year option at $206,560 — the rare case where the top-scoring and lowest-cost vendor are the same one, which made the recommendation easy to defend rather than a tradeoff to negotiate.

Recommendation & business impact

Select Northstar CRM. The full traceability matrix confirms all 14 functional requirements — including the two the sales team flagged as deal-breakers (territory pipeline visibility, quote-to-invoice handoff) — map to a satisfied Northstar capability, at a 3-year TCO $30-60K below the alternatives. The business requirements document gives Brightpath's sponsor a defensible, criteria-based selection record rather than a "the sales director liked it" decision — the kind of paper trail that matters when finance signs off on the contract.

The pitch in one line: "We need a CRM" isn't a decision — writing down what the business actually requires turned three similar-looking vendors into one clear, defensible answer.
