BA Case 03 · Change Management

Integrating two warehouse teams without it reading as a takeover

Stakeholder AnalysisRACIChange Plan

Brightpath Distribution has acquired Colton Regional Supply, an 85-employee regional distributor with its own warehouse systems and its own way of doing things. Two companies' warehouse operations need to merge onto one system and one reporting structure in four months — and every acquired employee's livelihood is riding on how that merge is handled. The systems-integration project plan is the easy part; the risk is that Colton's warehouse staff quietly keep using their old process while nodding along in training. This case builds the stakeholder map, RACI matrix, and communication plan meant to prevent that.

Approach

  1. 01

    Stakeholder identification — Mapped all 12 stakeholders across both companies (Brightpath and Colton) onto a power/interest grid, not just Brightpath's own leadership.

  2. 02

    RACI matrix — Defined who's Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed across 9 integration activities and 7 roles, validating every activity has exactly one accountable owner.

  3. 03

    Resistance risk register — Named the specific, realistic sources of resistance (a tenured Colton warehouse supervisor whose informal authority the new system routes around) rather than a generic "some employees may resist change" line.

  4. 04

    Communication plan — A 6-phase plan matched to the integration timeline, with different messages and channels for "Manage Closely" stakeholders versus "Keep Informed" ones.

Key finding

The obvious stakeholders — both companies' executive sponsors — are important but not the risk. The real risk sits with a Colton warehouse supervisor who has high influence over floor staff but wasn't in any of the planning conversations: high power, currently low interest, because nobody has engaged her yet. Left alone, that's exactly the profile that produces quiet non-adoption. The power/interest mapping put 4 of 12 stakeholders in "Manage Closely" — a deliberately tight list, because treating everyone as a top priority produces a plan that engages no one meaningfully.

Recommendation & business impact

Move the Colton warehouse supervisor from "not yet engaged" to "Manage Closely" before go-live, with a specific ask: co-lead the floor-level training rather than sit through it. The RACI matrix resolves the more mundane but equally real risk — overlapping ownership between the two companies' ops managers — by giving each of the 9 integration activities exactly one accountable owner. Together, these de-risk the part of an acquisition integration that budgets and project plans routinely miss: the people who have to actually change how they work every day.

The pitch in one line: The integration plan was fine — the risk was a warehouse supervisor with real influence and zero engagement, and no project plan on its own was going to catch that.
