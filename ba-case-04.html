Automate or keep hiring? A business case for order entry

Business CaseNPV / PaybackExcel

A routine headcount request for two more data-entry clerks at Brightpath Distribution (simulated) was never actually evaluated against the alternative — investing in OCR/RPA automation for the highest-volume order types. The instinct is to compare sticker prices, hire vs. buy — but a real business case has to account for what each option actually does to error rates, not just headcount cost.

3-yr cost: Hire
$1,592,000
3-yr cost: Automate
$1,512,000
NPV @ 8%
$165,288
Payback period
1.62 years

Approach

  1. 01

    Options framing — Defined Option B (hire 3 processors) and Option C (automate the highest-volume order types) against a documented baseline, with a full assumptions sheet (16 inputs) so every downstream number traces back to a stated, defensible assumption.

  2. 02

    Cost modeling — Built out 3-year fully loaded costs for each option: salary and overhead for hiring, license and implementation cost for automation.

  3. 03

    Second benefit lever — Modeled order-error-cost avoidance as its own line, not folded into "efficiency," because the pure headcount-cost difference between the two options is only about 5% — too thin on its own to be a credible business case.

  4. 04

    NPV & payback — Discounted cash flows at 8% year by year (not the built-in NPV shortcut, so every intermediate value is auditable) to get a real payback period, not a back-of-envelope one.

Key finding

On raw 3-year cost alone, automation wins by only about 5% ($1,512,000 vs. $1,592,000) — a margin thin enough that a skeptical finance reviewer could reasonably call it a wash once implementation risk is factored in. What changes the picture is the error-cost line: automation is projected to avoid $130,725 in order-error costs over the period that the hiring option wouldn't touch, because the error rate on high-volume order types is largely a data-entry problem, not a headcount problem. Combined, that's a $210,725 net benefit for automation — a real case, not a coin flip.

Recommendation & business impact

Recommend Option C (automate), on the combined case rather than headcount cost alone. NPV at an 8% discount rate is $165,288, with a payback period of 1.62 years — inside the horizon most capital committees will accept without extended debate. The business case document lays out both options honestly, including where automation's advantage is thin (raw cost) and where it's real (error avoidance), which is the difference between a business case that survives scrutiny and one that's a sales pitch.

The pitch in one line: The headcount math alone was nearly a tie — the case for automation only becomes clear once you count what it stops from going wrong, not just what it costs.
