Case 01 · Operations & Supply Chain

Cutting stockouts without carrying more inventory

SQLPythonDashboard

A mid-size distributor sets every SKU's reorder point the same way: a flat 3 weeks of average demand, regardless of how volatile that SKU's demand actually is or how long and unpredictable its supplier's lead time is. The result is a network fill rate stuck at 85.8%, well below the 95%+ typically targeted for revenue-critical items — and nobody can say which SKUs are driving it or what it's costing the business, only that “we're stocking out too much.”

Overall fill rate
85.8% → 98.9%
A-tier fill rate
88.4% → 99.1%
Extra carrying cost
$183K/yr
Net annual benefit
~$1.72M

Approach

  1. 01

    SQL — Rank all 180 SKUs by trailing revenue into A/B/C tiers with window functions, then measure fill rate and stockout rate by tier.

  2. 02

    Python — Compute a per-SKU safety stock and reorder point from each SKU's own demand volatility and its supplier's lead-time volatility — not a flat rule for everyone.

  3. 03

    Validate — Re-run the recommended reorder points through the same two-year demand simulation to get an actual before/after, not a theoretical one.

  4. 04

    Dashboard — The KPIs and charts a stakeholder would see in Power BI, built as a static, dependency-free HTML file.

Key finding

The SKUs most under-protected today cluster heavily in Electronics Accessories — the category with the longest, most variable supplier lead times, where the flat 3-week policy leaves reorder points 200–280% below what their own demand volatility calls for. The SKUs most over-protected sit in Office Supplies, where short, reliable domestic lead times mean the same flat policy runs 20–37% higher than necessary. A first pass targeting a single 95% service level everywhere was tried and rejected — it more than doubled network inventory investment, because the status quo turned out to be broadly under-protected rather than over-protected. The fix instead uses tier-differentiated service targets: protect the revenue-critical items tightly, accept more risk on the long tail.

Recommendation & business impact

Reset reorder points per SKU using tier-differentiated safety stock, funded by simultaneously trimming the over-protected Office Supplies SKUs. That requires roughly $832K more in average inventory investment, at an incremental carrying cost of about $183K/year — but it recovers an estimated $1.90M/year in gross margin currently lost to stockouts on revenue-critical items.

The pitch in one line: This isn't a cost-cutting story, it's a “the current policy is quietly losing $2M/year in sales it could have made” story — and the fix pays for itself roughly 9x over.
