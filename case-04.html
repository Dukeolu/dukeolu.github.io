Case 04 · HR & People Operations

Where the hiring funnel — and headcount — leaks

ExcelSQLPythonscikit-learnDashboard

Voluntary attrition has been running high enough that HR leadership wants to know two things: which departments are actually the problem, and whether the source of a hire has anything to do with how long they stay. Over the trailing period, 23.6% of the 1,230-employee cohort left voluntarily — the headline number everyone already has. The question this case answers is what's underneath it.

Overall attrition
23.6%
Job Board early attrition
21.3% vs. 6.8%
WH&Ops interview rate
30% vs. 48%
Net annual savings
~$202K

Approach

  1. 01

    SQL — Cut termination rate by tenure band and channel to show attrition isn't uniform over time; rank departments by termination rate alongside their average overtime and manager-span load.

  2. 02

    Python — A logistic regression ranks what structurally predicts attrition — tenure itself is deliberately excluded, since a terminated employee's tenure is mechanically how long until they left, which would make the model tautological rather than predictive.

  3. 03

    Excel — A live-formula workbook (645 formulas, zero errors): a monthly headcount roll-forward, the full recruiting funnel with a channel conversion-rate heatmap, and the early-attrition cost scenario.

  4. 04

    Dashboard — A funnel-stage comparison chart isolating exactly where Warehouse & Ops' Job Board pipeline falls behind the network average.

Key finding

Department-level attrition looks like it points one direction — Marketing (28.2%) and Warehouse & Ops (28.1%) both run highest — but only one has a structural explanation in this data: Warehouse & Ops carries the highest overtime load (14.0 hrs/mo vs. 5–6 across the rest of the business) and the widest average manager span. The channel finding is sharper: Job Board hires leave within their first 90 days at 21.3%, versus 6.8% for Employee Referral — more than 3x. Job Board is also Warehouse & Ops' dominant sourcing channel, and its pipeline there has a second, independent problem: screening throughput matches the network average, but conversion from screened to interviewed collapses to 30%, versus 48% network-wide — a pure bottleneck, not a candidate-quality issue.

Recommendation & business impact

Two fixes, both traced to the same channel, neither requiring a network-wide policy change. Shift sourcing mix away from Job Board toward Employee Referral where volume allows — at Employee Referral's early-attrition rate, Job Board's 310 hires over 24 months would have produced roughly 45 fewer early departures. And clear the Warehouse & Ops screening backlog: at the network's normal Job Board conversion rate, the department should be producing materially more interviews than it is — about 34 missed interviews a year of otherwise-viable candidates.

The pitch in one line: The attrition problem isn't spread evenly across the business — it's concentrated in one hiring channel and one department's broken pipeline, and fixing those two things recovers roughly $200K a year.
