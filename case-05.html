Case 05 · Energy & Investment Analytics

Where would battery storage pay off?

SQLPythonForecastingDashboard

Meridian Renewables (simulated) operates five renewable sites — three solar, two onshore wind — and has budget for one behind-the-meter battery storage investment this year. Every site "could" host a battery; the decision the business actually needs to make is which site first, and whether the economics of the best candidate actually clear an investment hurdle.

Solar capture rate
76.3%
Wind capture rate
106-107%
Top-site battery uplift
£216,474/yr
Recommended investment
NPV £2.72M · 3.4-yr payback

Approach

  1. 01

    SQL — Capacity factor and "capture rate" by site (volume-weighted price received ÷ simple average market price), plus a query isolating the cannibalisation mechanism directly: average price by national-solar-output quintile.

  2. 02

    Python — A battery arbitrage simulation per site: a co-located 10 MW/20 MWh battery time-shifts each site's own generation from its cheapest hours to its priciest hours daily, ranking all five sites by annual revenue uplift.

  3. 03

    Forecasting — A seasonal-naive forecast of the monthly price spread validated against a second year of data (3.4% mean error), used to check the arbitrage opportunity is stable enough to underwrite a 15-year asset against.

  4. 04

    Investment modeling — 15-year NPV/IRR/payback for the top- and bottom-ranked sites, stacking arbitrage revenue with a capacity-market payment, the way a real battery investment is actually underwritten.

Key finding

All three solar sites capture only 76.3% of the simple average market price for their output, while the two wind sites capture 106-107% — a ~31 percentage-point gap driven entirely by when each technology generates, not by how much. Every solar site generates at once, at midday, which is exactly what suppresses the price at that hour: average price falls from £35.2/MWh in the lowest quintile of national solar output to £16.0/MWh in the highest. And the sites earning the least per MWh gain the most from a battery — estimated annual arbitrage uplift ranges from £216,474/year at Solent Solar Park down to £146,451/year at Cambria Wind Farm, tracking capture-rate disadvantage rather than nameplate capacity.

Recommendation & business impact

Fund the first battery at Solent Solar Park, not at the largest site by nameplate capacity — the arbitrage-value ranking should decide the order, not total generation volume. Pure wholesale arbitrage alone doesn't clear a normal investment hurdle at any site, but stacked with capacity-market revenue, Solent's case is clearly the strongest: NPV £2,720,122, IRR 28.5%, payback 3.4 years, versus NPV £2,182,925, IRR 24.7%, and a 3.9-year payback at the lowest-ranked site — a £537,197 NPV gap driven entirely by the arbitrage layer.

The pitch in one line: Battery storage is worth the most exactly where the market is paying renewable output the least — and that's a rankable, quantifiable question, not a guess.
